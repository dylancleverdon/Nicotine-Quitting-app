# Firewatch: vision and principles

Baastik Labs · updated 28 Sep 2026

## The vision

**Firewatch puts you in control of your nicotine use, instead of it controlling you.**

It's for anyone who uses nicotine. In order of priority, it does three things:

1. **Find.** Show where your addiction really is: a clear number instead of a guess, as fast as possible.
2. **Control.** Help keep it from creeping up, and celebrate holding steady. Staying at a steady,
   controlled level is a real achievement and a perfectly good outcome.
3. **Taper, if you want.** When you're ready, help you step down, all the way to Clear Air
   (nicotine-free) if you choose. Clear Air is always within arm's reach and never downplayed, but
   never forced.

The order matters. When features compete, finding the real level comes before holding it, and
holding it comes before tapering. That's why new users start at an early estimate of 8 pieces a
day, and why a step-up is offered the same day a day runs heavy: people should find the level
they're really at quickly, instead of struggling at one they aren't.

Firewatch is a private tool. It should be easy and painless to use every day, never make anyone
feel judged, and always be waiting, with no guilt, whenever someone is ready to pick it back up.
The app works for the user, not the other way around.

## Principles

Every feature has to follow these.

1. **Honest number first.** Where you really are beats where you'd like to be. Estimates are
   always labelled as estimates.
2. **Control, not pressure.** Holding steady is a win. Tapering is offered ("or stay here, that's
   fine too"), never pushed. A step down is earned, not on demand: it unlocks once a level has
   been held, and then it stays unlocked (a button in Insights) until you take it or your level
   changes, so you can hold a little longer and step down when it suits you.
3. **Never judge.**
   - Tone: plain and factual by default; a calm, encouraging coach for wins; neutral, like a tool,
     for misses.
   - Never condescending. Never spin a miss positively ("you'll get 'em next time" is out).
   - Never show a negative comparison ("you were at Embers last week, now you're at Bonfire").
     Motivating figures only ever show wins.
   - Stepping up is always allowed and is framed as the app getting more accurate.
4. **Easy to come back.** After a break the app just resumes. A quiet "Welcome back" offers quick
   back-dating, day by day, with no times needed.
5. **Tiny learning curve.** Simple on day one and on day three hundred. Anything advanced is opt-in
   and lives in Settings.
6. **The app must never become a trigger.** Logging takes about two seconds. Nothing to press
   during a craving. The next-piece timer can be hidden.
7. **A tool, not a nag.** The only notifications are in the opt-in Relapse prevention mode, so
   people never have a reason to switch notifications off.
8. **Private.** Nothing is shareable. Sharing invites judgement, and judgement makes it harder to
   come back after a rough patch.
9. **Harm reduction without lectures.** Any product can be tracked, cigarettes included; the
   quality score reflects the method. Switching from cigarettes or vapes to gum or pouches is a
   big deal and worth celebrating. A relapse means cigarettes or vapes, not gum or pouches.
   Practical tips about delivery (like bridging with gum) only appear for people who switch on
   Coaching tips: opting in is permission to coach a bit.
10. **Back-dating without times by default.** People rarely remember exact times. Rough counts per
    day are spread across the waking day; an exact time is optional.

## What success looks like

- A new user sees a useful level on day one and their real level within a week.
- Someone who only wants to hold steady feels good about the app, not nagged by it.
- Someone who wants to quit can see Clear Air getting closer and step down one small rung at a time.
- Someone who drifts away for weeks comes back, taps "Welcome back", and carries on without guilt.
